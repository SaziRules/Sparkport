Sparkport Pharmacy
What you need to know about S5 and S6 controlled substances
================================================================================

South African medicines are classified into schedules under the Medicines and Related
Substances Act 101 of 1965. Schedule 5 and Schedule 6 substances are habit-forming
or potentially dangerous and are subject to strict dispensing controls regulated by
SAHPRA.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SCHEDULE OVERVIEW
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  S0–S2 | Over-the-counter medicines
         No prescription required. Issued at the discretion of the pharmacist.

  S3–S4 | Medicine requiring a Doctor's prescription
         Requires a valid prescription issued by a registered practitioner.

  S5    | Habit-forming substances  ⚠ CONTROLLED
         Prescription required. Original script must be presented to the pharmacist.
         Up to 5 repeats allowed within 6 months.

  S6    | Potentially dangerous substances  ⛔ STRICTLY CONTROLLED
         Prescription required. Original script must be presented to the pharmacist
         and is retained after dispensing. No repeats permitted under any
         circumstances.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR PRESCRIPTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Bring the original
  S5 and S6 medications require your original prescription. We cannot accept
  photographs, copies, or faxed prescriptions for these substances.

S5 repeat limits
  If your medication is a Schedule 5 substance, your prescription may be repeated
  up to 5 times within a 6-month period from the date of issue, as permitted by law.

S6: no repeats
  Schedule 6 medications cannot be repeated under any circumstances. The pharmacist
  is legally required to retain the original prescription after dispensing.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VERIFICATION PROCESS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  1. Our pharmacist reviews your submission and identifies any scheduled substances.

  2. We may contact your prescribing practitioner to verify authenticity.

  3. For S5 and S6 substances, your original prescription is required — no copies,
     photographs, or faxed prescriptions are accepted.

  4. Once dispensed, your S6 prescription is retained by us as required by law.
     S5 prescriptions may be returned to allow for authorised repeats.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

YOUR RESPONSIBILITIES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

By submitting a prescription containing scheduled substances, you confirm:

  ✓ The prescription was issued by a registered South African healthcare practitioner
    (registered with the HPCSA).

  ✓ You have not submitted the same prescription to another pharmacy for simultaneous
    dispensing.

  ✓ For S5 and S6 medications, you understand the original prescription must be
    presented to the pharmacist. S6 prescriptions are retained after dispensing.

REGULATORY BODIES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  SAHPRA — South African Health Products Regulatory Authority
    Website: sahpra.org.za
    Regulates medicines, medical devices, and related matters in South Africa.

  SAPC — South African Pharmacy Council
    Website: pharmcouncil.co.za
    Governs the practice of pharmacy and protects public health through professional
    standards.

  HPCSA — Health Professions Council of South Africa
    Website: hpcsa.co.za
    Registers and regulates healthcare practitioners.

  National Department of Health
    Website: health.gov.za
    Responsible for national health policy and the Medicines Act.
